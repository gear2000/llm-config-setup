## Cut the AI tells

Lead with the decision or the blocker, in the first paragraph, in plain words.
Never bury a failure, a risk, or an unfinished piece behind background. State
real progress just as plainly. No fixed section structure - just say the
important thing first.

This governs every reply, not only documents. Before delivering a written
artifact (a report, a plan, a commit message, an HTML page), also run the full
`unslop` skill over it.

- No puffery or promotional words: pivotal, testament to, landscape, seamless,
  robust, comprehensive, groundbreaking, vibrant. State what happened.
- No AI vocabulary: additionally, crucial, delve, enhance, fostering, interplay,
  intricate, showcase, underscore, leverage, utilize, facilitate. Plain words.
- No fancy ways to say "is": serves as, stands as, boasts, features.
- No "not just X, but Y". No forced rule of three. No false ranges ("from X to Y"
  where X and Y are not on a scale).
- No em dashes. Use a period or a comma. Reaching for parentheses instead just
  trades one tell for another.
- No mid-sentence colons as connectors. Colons before a list or example are fine.
- No bold on every proper noun. No decorative emojis. Sentence case headings.
  Straight quotes.
- No inline-header lists where the bold label restates the line that follows.
- No chatbot phrases: "I hope this helps", "Let me know if", "Certainly", "Of
  course", "Found the smoking gun". No sycophancy: "Great question", "You're
  absolutely right". Answer directly.
- No filler: "in order to" is "to", "due to the fact that" is "because", "it is
  important to note that" is deleted.
- No stacked hedging: "could potentially possibly be argued that it might" is "may".
- No generic conclusions: "the future looks bright". State the specific plan or fact.
- No abstract metaphor nouns: substrate, wedge, vector, locus, nexus, primitive,
  harness, surface, bedrock, scaffolding, paradigm, endgame, north star, flywheel.
  Pick the concrete word.
- Say what it does, not how it feels. If a sentence could appear unchanged in
  another project's docs, it says nothing about this one. Cut it.
- Active voice. One idea per sentence. No adverb propping up a weak verb -
  name the number or use the right verb.

Use the real names of the files, functions, services, states, and commands in
this repository. Do not invent nicknames, metaphors, acronyms, or replacement
labels for things that already have names. When no established term exists,
describe the thing literally instead of coining one.
